A more human way to use AI in marketing

Good automation makes repetitive work lighter without handing over your judgement.

RRizqOne EditorialOctober 8, 20264 min read
HUMAN-LED
Brief→Assist→Review
PEOPLE MAKE THE CALL

AI can help with repetitive marketing tasks, but faster is not automatically better. A workflow is only useful when it produces work your team can stand behind—and gives people a clear way to catch errors before they matter.

Start with a real bottleneck

Before selecting a tool, notice where time is going. Is your team repeatedly gathering the same public information? Reformatting incoming briefs? Sorting requests? Creating a first outline from material a specialist has already checked?

Choose a contained task with a clear input and a useful output. If the task needs sensitive information, expert judgement or a nuanced relationship with a customer, the workflow needs more care—or may be better kept entirely with a person.

Make the handoffs visible

A practical workflow tells everyone what happens at each step. Someone supplies the brief and context. A tool may assist with a first pass. A person then checks whether the result is accurate, on-brand, appropriate and genuinely helpful. Only after that review should work move to a customer-facing or publishing stage.

  • Be explicit about the task. Give the workflow a narrow, testable job rather than a vague request to “do the marketing”.
  • Set limits. Decide what data can be used and what types of output must not be generated or sent automatically.
  • Keep an accountable reviewer. A named person should be able to correct, reject or escalate the output.
  • Test edge cases. Check incomplete inputs, unusual requests and inaccurate drafts before relying on the process.

Protect trust and privacy

Understand how a tool stores and processes information before connecting it to business data. Don’t feed personal, confidential or customer information into a system unless your organisation has reviewed the tool and approved that use. Follow the relevant privacy, security and content policies that apply to your work.

Be honest about the role of automation wherever disclosure is appropriate. Do not use generated material to fabricate testimonials, expertise, citations or evidence.

Measure the quality, not just the speed

Track whether the workflow is reducing a real burden and whether quality remains acceptable. Time saved is useful only if the review process, corrections and downstream issues do not consume that time again.

Start small, compare assisted work with your existing process, and collect feedback from the people who use it. Keep what helps. Change or remove what creates extra work.
