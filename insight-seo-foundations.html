The SEO foundations worth getting right

Before the next tactic, make sure your site can be found, understood and used.

RRizqOne EditorialOctober 14, 20265 min read
01FOUND
YOUR
GROUND

It is tempting to look for the next SEO trick. But if a search engine cannot access an important page—or a visitor cannot tell what the page is for—another tactic may not solve the real problem.

Start with access and clarity

Review whether the pages that matter are available to visitors and can be discovered through your site’s links. Check that each important page has a clear purpose, a descriptive title and headings that help a reader follow the information.

Search engines use many signals to decide what to show. There is no single checklist that guarantees rankings. Still, a site that communicates clearly and works reliably gives both people and systems a better place to start.

Make the page useful to the person who arrives

For each priority page, ask what the visitor needs to understand or do next. A useful service page describes the work, who it is for, how it may help and what a sensible next step looks like. Avoid repeating a phrase just to make it appear more often.

  • Match the page to an intent. A person researching a topic may need an explanation; a person comparing providers may need specifics about your service.
  • Use plain, specific language. Explain your offering in words your customers recognise.
  • Link related pages. Help readers find useful next steps, and make the structure of your site easier to follow.
  • Check mobile and accessibility. Ensure text is readable, controls are usable and important content works across screen sizes.

Look after the technical basics

Page speed, mobile layout, secure connections, crawl access and redirects can all affect a site’s usability or discoverability. Which issue matters most depends on your site, so begin with a review rather than making changes blindly.

Keep your page titles and descriptions accurate. Use structured data only when it describes content that is genuinely present on the page. A sitemap can help communicate which URLs you want crawled, but it does not guarantee that every URL will be indexed.

Measure what helps you make a decision

Choose a small set of indicators tied to your goals. Depending on your business, these might include qualified enquiries from organic search, engagement with a key service page, or how important pages appear for relevant searches. An increase in visits alone does not always mean growth.

Record a baseline, make focused changes, then give search engines and people time to respond. Review outcomes and decide what to do next. SEO works best as ongoing learning—not a promise of a specific position by a specific date.
